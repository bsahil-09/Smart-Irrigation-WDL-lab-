Irrigation Workflow Steps

The system follows a automated sequential workflow to ensure precise water management for sugarcane fields:

  1. Real-Time Field Data Collection: IoT field sensors continuously measure ground conditions.
  2. Data Transmission: Collected values are sent wirelessly to the central agricultural cloud database.
  3. AI Analysis & Modeling: AI algorithms compute soil water deficits based on crop stage and weather inputs.
  4. Advisory Generation: System flags fields needing water with HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW priority levels.
  5. Automated Action / Alert: Farmers receive push notifications or auto-drip valves trigger for controlled watering.

Sensors Used in the System

Our field deployment utilizes standard high-precision sensor units across the sugarcane farm:

  • Soil Moisture Sensor: Measures volumetric water content in soil around root zones.
  • Temperature Sensor: Tracks ambient field temperature to calculate potential evapotranspiration rates.
  • Humidity Sensor: Monitors relative atmospheric humidity to assess microclimate stress.
  • Rainfall Sensor: Detects live precipitation to automatically override scheduled watering events.

Sugarcane Crop Stages & Water Needs

Water requirements vary significantly across different phases of sugarcane growth. Below is a detailed breakdown:

Germination Stage (0 to 30 Days)
This phase involves seed cane sprouting and root initiation. It requires light but frequent irrigation to keep soil moist without drowning young roots.
Tillering Stage (30 to 120 Days)
Shoots multiply rapidly. Water stress during this phase reduces total stalk count, so maintaining consistent adequate moisture is vital.
Grand Growth Stage (120 to 270 Days)
This is the most critical water consumption phase where cane height and girth build up. Requires maximum irrigation depth for optimal biomass yield.
Maturity Stage (270 to 360 Days)
Sucrose accumulates in the stalks. Irrigation is intentionally tapered off and withheld 15–30 days before harvest to maximize sugar density.
